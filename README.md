# The Paradox of Innovation
*A Theory of Convergence* — a poster series by Abhishek Choudhary

> Innovation is not merely divergence. It is convergence after discovery.

Nine plates and a cover, arguing that the capacity to explore without constraint while converging
with precision is a **scale-sensitive property**, not a personality trait — and proposing
**inclination** as the primitive current cognitive architectures may be missing.

## The core claim

Inclination is a **signed directional disposition over possible transitions**, valued in [−1, +1],
attached to the transition structure rather than to states.

- `0` is directional **neutrality**, not cessation.
- Negative inclination is **active counter-inclination**, not the absence of positive inclination.
- Decay weakens **magnitude** while preserving **sign**; reversal requires new information.

It is not a gradient (a derivative field) and not a probability (an expectational quantity). It sits
between Evaluation and Selection in the PEDLER chain, and the **Natural Justice Gate** stands between
emerging directional preference and executable adverse transition:

> Inclination is evidence of directional disposition, not authorization to execute.

## Four parts

1. **The Epistemic Operations** — three modes (divergent, documentary, convergent), and what
   citation metrics actually measure: institutional realization, not divergent capacity.
2. **The Phenotype** — globally divergent, locally convergent; and the observer's burden, where the
   interpretive error may lie in the observer's model rather than the person's cognition.
3. **Inclination as Primitive** — judicial reasoning, backpropagation and PEDLER share one
   abstraction; neuroscience correlates are offered as correlation, not proof.
4. **The Theory of Convergence** — CEMs by substrate, and convergence as constraint-induced
   contraction of a signed transition field.
